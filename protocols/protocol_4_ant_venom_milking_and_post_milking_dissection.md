# Venom milking

## Purpose

Milking of ant venom for downstream molecular work and for proteomic analysis.

- Ice box filled with ice (Where: S1 Laboratory, room 6.01.0); dry ice (Where: S1 Laboratory, room E.01.0)

## Procedure

1. Cool ants on ice, either placing them directly on ice or placing them on ice in a petri-dish

2. Wait until the ants are unconscious (do not place too many ants on ice and not for too long, i.e. Camponotus is unwilling to give venom if too long on ice)

3. Clean the ant holding device before starting to work with water and ethanol. If venom spills on the holding device during milking clean between ants. Clean at least once properly when changing ant colony to avoid risk of venom contamination.

4. Label glass vial with your name, date, ant colony name, amount of venom at the end of the collection procedure and place in ice/dry-ice (ice is ok if the venom is not left for too long ~2h, otherwise place on dry ice)

5. Firmly fix ant in the ant holding device under the stereomicroscope with good accessibility to gaster tip/acidoporus

6. Wait until ant is completely awake in the ant holding device, then poke ant gaster gently with forceps in one hand while holding the microcapillary in a “peleusball” at the tip of the gaster/acidoporus

7. As soon as ant has expelled venom and venom has been taken up in the microcapillary, transfer venom into the prepared glass tube. The microcapillary can be re-used between ants of the same colony but must be changed between colonies to avoid venom contamination! In case the microcapillary does not draw well venom any more or if

8. Repeat step 6 and 7, three to four times until the ant has expelled all its venom

9. Take note how much venom you have collected (microcapillaries are graded to give you an idea how much you collected).

10. Collect venom of at least 20 (Formica) and 40 (Lasius) ant individuals per colony into one glass tube: for proteomic analysis we aim for ~2µl of venom per ant colony.

11. Put collected venom at −80°C (Where: S1 Laboratory, room E.01.0).

12. Milked individual ants of one colony are placed into small nest boxes with water and honey for venom gland dissection 2-3 days (Sarah: you did now always 2, or not? Lukas I think did always 3?) after milking (some ants will die from the shock of the procedure, thus prepare more than you will need for dissection)

# Venom gland dissection

## Purpose

Dissection of ant venom glands for downstream molecular work.

## Materials and where to find them

- Ant specimens (What: formicine ants, Where: ant room, 1st floor, room 1.16.0; incubator contents: [Ant incubator inventory](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1NVImQQ3s4nkkv7QC7YHEKTI-5tz0e2wVJwapN9ZLL9U/edit?gid=1206572294#gid=1206572294))

- Fine forceps (What: Dumond No 5, Where: XXXX)

- Dissection surface (What: Microscope slides, Where: XXXX)

- Ice box filled with ice (Where: S1 Laboratory, room 6.01.0); dry ice (Where: S1 Laboratory, room E.01.0)

- RNA-preservation solution (What: Zymo Research DNA/RNA Shield, ready-to-use, Cat. No. R1100-250, 250 mL; store stock at room temperature, not in the fridge; do not pipette directly from stock bottle. Prepare a separate working aliquot. Working volumes used: 50 µL per individual on microscope slide; 100 µL initially per sample collection tube. Where: exact stock storage location to be confirmed) or other storage medium, depending on downstream application

- Booked stereomicroscope workstation (Where: Bee Lab, room 6.15.0; booking via Google Calendar: [Google Calendar](https://calendar.google.com/calendar/u/0/r))

- Pipettes, pipette tips and 2 mL Eppendorf tubes (What: Eppendorf Research plus pipette, 20–200 µL, labeled “Bee Lab”; pipette tips and 2 mL tubes: details to be confirmed. Where: pipette in Bee Lab, room 6.15.0; tips and tubes: XXXX)

## Procedure

1. Cool ants (those that were milked 2-3 days before) on ice, either placing them directly on ice or placing them on ice in a petri-dish and wait until the ants are unconscious

2. Label two **2-mL** Eppendorf tubes with your name, date, ant colony name, type (carcass and venom gland tissue) and number of tissues (Formica: ~15, Lasius: **~30).** **Add** **100** **µL** **DNA/RNA** **Shield** **to** **each sample collection tube** and place in ice/dry-ice (ice is ok if tissues are not left for too **long,** **~2 h;** otherwise place on dry **ice).**

3. Clean stereomicroscope area and microscope slide with water and ethanol. Clean microscope slide between ants. Clean at least once properly everything again when changing ant colony to avoid risk of contamination.

4. Place unconscious ant on microscope slide and remove the head and thorax.

5. Place head and thorax in the carcass tube

6. Pipette **50** **µL** **DNA/RNA** **Shield** **onto** **the** microscope slide** for each individual ant** and place the remaining abdominal section on its back. This orientation makes the internal tissues easier to access and grasp.** Whether dissection solution is transferred with the tissue to the collection tube remains to be specified.**

7. Carefully remove or open the cuticle at the posterior end of the abdomen.

8. Locate the cord-like tissue in the terminal abdominal region and gently grasp it with fine forceps. (not sure about this step as I do not understand what the cord like tissue should be

9. Pull the tissue out slowly and carefully, maintaining the in-situ location of the venom gland tissue described in 10 (all attached to the last abdominal segment/gaster tip)

10. Identify the venom apparatus by its characteristic morphology plus associated tissues:

- the venom reservoir is translucent and balloon-like;

- the associated venom duct ends at the gaster tip/acidoporus

- on top of the reservoir lies the convoluted gland as a milky structure

- from the convoluted gland two fine filaments that are freely floating in the haemolypmph emerge.

- Associated with the venom gland tissues (reservoir, duct, convoluted gland and filaments) is the Dufour gland (unilobed or bilobed and darker in colour) that also opens into the duct.

- The hindgut also empties into the acidoporus near the venom gland tissues

- Fat bodies appear glomerular in the vicinity of the venom tissue while trachea appear tube like

11. Once the in-situ location of all the venom gland tissue has been determined (reservoir: empty or still partly full? Filaments attached to the convoluted gland or lost?), separate the venom gland apparatus from surrounding tissues as cleanly as possible (at this stage it does not matter whether the reservoir is ruptured ant venom spills out. This might be even beneficial for the RNA-extraction).

12. Transfer the dissected venom gland tissues and the rest of the abdominal tissue immediately to the appropriate collection tube

13. For RNA-extraction ~15 (Formica) and ~30 (Lasius) venom gland tissue samples and associated carcasses should be pooled per colony.

## Practical notes

- Pull the tissue gently. The venom reservoir and associated structures can tear easily.

- The terminal abdominal region contains several tissues close together, so contamination with surrounding tissue is possible and should be avoided.

- Glass plates can make the dissection easier because they provide a flat surface, but clean them thoroughly between samples to avoid contamination risk.

- For RNA work, minimize handling time and transfer dissected tissue to preservation solution as quickly as possible.

## Quality control

A successful dissection should yield:

- an intact or mostly intact translucent venom reservoir;

- attached duct/glandular tissue;

- minimal visible contamination from surrounding abdominal tissues or Dufour's gland.
