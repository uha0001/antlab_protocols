# RNA Extraction Protocol
### by Lukas Koch

## Purpose

RNA extraction from dissected ant venom glands and corresponding carcass samples using a TRIzol/chloroform extraction followed by isopropanol precipitation.

## Starting Material

- Dissected venom glands and/or carcass tissue stored in RNA-preservation solution
- 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes

For small ant species, multiple glands may need to be pooled to obtain sufficient RNA.

## Reagents

- PBS
- RLT buffer with beta-mercaptoethanol
- TRIzol
- Chloroform
- Glycogen
- Isopropanol
- 75% ethanol, ice-cold
- Nuclease-free water

## Equipment

- 2 mL microcentrifuge tubes
- 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes
- 5 mm steel beads
- TissueLyser or equivalent homogenizer
- Refrigerated centrifuge
- Vortex mixer
- Ice
- pH paper
- Heat block or incubator

## Procedure

### 1. Wash the Sample

1. Transfer the sample to a 2 mL microcentrifuge tube.
2. Add **200 µL PBS**.
3. Vortex briefly.
4. Centrifuge to pellet the tissue 1 min, 12000 g.
5. Discard the supernatant.
6. Check the pH of the discarded wash using pH paper if required.
7. Repeat the wash if necessary until the sample is sufficiently free of preservation solution.

> RLT can also be used during the initial preparation depending on the sample and workflow.

### 2. Tissue Homogenization

1. Add **100 µL RLT buffer containing beta-mercaptoethanol** to the tissue.
2. Add **one 5 mm steel bead**.
3. Homogenize using the TissueLyser at approximately **50 Hz**:
   - 30 s homogenization
   - 1 min on ice
   - repeat for a total of **4 cycles**
4. If the homogenate contains a large amount of debris, optionally centrifuge at approximately **12,000 × g for 1 min**.
5. Transfer approximately **100 µL supernatant/homogenate** to a fresh tube.

### 3. TRIzol Extraction

1. Add **1 mL TRIzol** to approximately **100 µL homogenate**.
2. Vortex thoroughly.
3. Incubate for **5 min**.
4. Add **200 µL chloroform**.
5. Vortex for approximately **30 s**.
6. Allow the sample to stand for approximately **3 min** to begin phase separation.
7. Centrifuge for **15 min at approximately 12,000 × g at 4°C**.
8. Carefully transfer the **upper aqueous phase** to a fresh 1.5 mL tube without disturbing the interface.

### 4. RNA Precipitation

1. Add **2 µL glycogen** to the recovered aqueous phase.
2. Add **600 µL isopropanol**.
3. Mix by gently inverting the tube approximately **20 times**.
4. Incubate **overnight at −20°C**.

### 5. Pellet RNA

1. Centrifuge the sample for **20 min at 4°C** at high speed (handwritten notes indicate approximately **30,000 × g**; verify the intended setting before use).
2. Carefully discard the supernatant without disturbing the RNA pellet.

### 6. Ethanol Wash

1. Add approximately **1 mL ice-cold 75% ethanol** to the pellet.
2. Centrifuge again for approximately **10 min at 4°C**.
3. Remove the ethanol carefully.
4. If residual ethanol remains, perform a brief additional spin to collect it at the bottom of the tube.
5. Remove the remaining ethanol with an appropriately sized pipette tip.

### 7. Dry and Resuspend RNA

1. Air-dry the pellet for approximately **10 min at 36°C**.
2. Resuspend the RNA pellet in nuclease-free water.

Example elution volumes used during pilot work:

- **Venom-gland samples:** approximately **12 µL**
- **Carcass samples:** approximately **50 µL**

Adjust the elution volume according to expected RNA yield and downstream requirements.

## RNA Quality Control

RNA quality and concentration can be assessed using an RNA ScreenTape/TapeStation assay or equivalent method.

Record:

- RNA concentration
- RIN/RINe
- total elution volume
- sample type
- number of glands/individuals pooled

## Notes and Troubleshooting

- Avoid transferring material from the interphase during the aqueous-phase transfer.
- Very small pellets may be difficult to see; glycogen helps with precipitation and pellet visibility.
- Do not overdry the RNA pellet, as this may make resuspension difficult.
- If dissected tissues are not completely clean, RNA from surrounding tissues may contribute to the sample.
- For small ant species, pooling more venom glands may be necessary.
