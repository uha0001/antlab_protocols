# Venom Gland Dissection

## Purpose

Dissection of ant venom glands for downstream molecular work.

> **Working protocol:** This version is transcribed and cleaned up from handwritten lab notes. Confirm species-specific anatomy and sample handling requirements before use.

## Materials

- Ant specimens
- Fine forceps
- Dissection surface
- Glass plate or suitable dissection dish
- Appropriate collection tubes
- RNA-preservation solution or other storage medium, depending on downstream application

## Procedure

1. Remove the head.
2. Remove the second body section / anterior abdominal section, leaving the terminal abdominal region for dissection.
3. Place the remaining abdominal section on its back. This orientation makes the internal tissues easier to access and grasp.
4. Carefully remove or open the cuticle at the posterior end of the abdomen.
5. Locate the cord-like tissue in the terminal abdominal region and gently grasp it with fine forceps.
6. Pull the tissue out slowly and carefully.
7. Identify the venom apparatus by its characteristic morphology:
   - the venom reservoir is translucent and balloon-like;
   - the associated duct and convoluted gland/filaments are attached to the reservoir.
8. Separate the venom gland apparatus from surrounding tissues as cleanly as possible.
9. Transfer the dissected tissue immediately to the appropriate collection tube or preservation buffer.

## Tissue Identification Notes

### Venom gland

The venom reservoir appears as a **translucent, balloon-like sac** connected to a duct and convoluted glandular tissue.

### Dufour's gland

The Dufour's gland is located close to the venom apparatus and appears **darker in color**. Take care not to include it unintentionally when clean venom-gland tissue is required.

## Practical Notes

- Pull the tissue gently. The venom reservoir and associated structures can tear easily.
- The terminal abdominal region contains several tissues close together, so contamination with surrounding tissue is possible.
- Glass plates can make the dissection easier because they provide a flat surface, but consider contamination risk and clean them thoroughly between samples.
- For RNA work, minimize handling time and transfer dissected tissue to preservation solution as quickly as possible.

## Quality Control

A successful dissection should yield:

- an intact or mostly intact translucent venom reservoir;
- attached duct/glandular tissue;
- minimal visible contamination from surrounding abdominal tissues or Dufour's gland.
