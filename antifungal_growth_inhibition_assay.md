# Antifungal Growth Inhibition Assay

## Purpose

Preparation of a fungal spore suspension and setup of a microplate-based antifungal growth inhibition assay.

> **Working protocol:** This document is transcribed from handwritten assay notes. Confirm the exact fungal strain, incubation conditions, detection-reagent conditions, and final plate volumes before running the assay.

## General Notes

- Use **sterile filter tips throughout**.
- Prepare the fungal working suspension **last**, because spores can germinate quickly.
- Use sterile technique throughout the assay.

## Materials

- Fungal culture on Petri dish
- Triton X solution
- Sterile-filtered RPMI medium
- Test compounds/peptides
- DMSO
- Detection reagent, if used
- Neubauer counting chamber
- 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tubes
- 96-well plate
- Sterile water

## 1. Harvest Fungal Spores

1. Add approximately **900 µL Triton X** to the Petri dish containing the fungal culture.
2. Add the liquid slowly because the fungal surface may be hydrophobic and the liquid can splash.
3. Move the liquid gently across the surface of the Petri dish to collect spores.
4. Transfer the resulting spore suspension to a **1.5 mL tube**.

## 2. Prepare a 1:10 Dilution for Counting

1. Prepare a **1:10 dilution**:
   - **100 µL spore suspension**
   - **900 µL Triton X**
2. Vortex thoroughly.
3. Load approximately **10 µL** onto a Neubauer counting chamber.
4. Count spores in the inner counting area.

Use the chamber count and dilution factor to calculate the spore concentration.

## 3. Prepare the Working Spore Suspension

The target fungal working suspension is approximately:

**10^4 spores/mL**

Dilute the counted spore suspension accordingly.

The final working suspension should be prepared in **sterile-filtered RPMI** shortly before plate setup.

## 4. Prepare Media and Controls

Prepare the required solutions before preparing the final fungal suspension.

Possible stocks/controls include:

- RPMI only
- RPMI + detection reagent
- RPMI + fungal spores
- RPMI + DMSO

A **2% DMSO** condition was noted for compounds/peptides with poor solubility.

## 5. Prepare Compound Dilutions

Prepare the starting compound concentration and perform serial dilutions.

The handwritten working series was:

**64 → 32 → 16 → 8 → 4 → 2 → 1 → 0.5 → 0.25 mM**

Use the concentration range appropriate for the specific compound and experiment.

Prepare sufficient volume for triplicate wells and controls.

## 6. 96-Well Plate Setup

1. Fill the outer wells with approximately **100 µL sterile water** to reduce edge effects.
2. Arrange compound concentrations across the internal wells.
3. Run each concentration in **triplicate**.
4. Include appropriate positive and negative controls.

Example control notes:

- **Positive control:** fungal spore suspension in RPMI without test compound
- **Negative control:** medium without spores

The handwritten notes indicate approximately **25 µL** for some control additions; verify the final assay volume and plate map before use.

## 7. Add Fungal Suspension

Prepare the final fungal suspension immediately before addition to the plate.

Add the required volume of fungal suspension to the appropriate wells so that the final assay contains the intended spore concentration.

Avoid unnecessary delays because spores may begin germinating.

## 8. Detection and Readout

Add the detection reagent according to the assay design and reagent instructions.

Record:

- fungal strain
- starting spore concentration
- final spore concentration per well
- compound concentrations
- DMSO concentration
- plate layout
- incubation time and temperature
- detection reagent
- plate-reader settings

## Plate Layout Notes

- Use outer wells for sterile water when possible to reduce evaporation/edge effects.
- Keep triplicates together and use a consistent serial-dilution direction.
- Clearly identify positive and negative controls.
